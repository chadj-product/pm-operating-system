# Output standards (always on)

Four rules. They apply to every artifact this system produces.

1. **Every planning artifact declares success metrics and open questions.** A metric needs a
   baseline, a target, and the mechanism that will measure it. A named open question is a
   managed risk; an unnamed one is a surprise.

2. **Every requirement carries Gherkin acceptance criteria.** Given / When / Then, with a
   concrete precondition and an observable outcome.

3. **Numbers carry units and a source.** Every figure in a document that reaches another human
   is either cited or explicitly marked as an estimate with its basis.

4. **Unknowns are labeled, never smoothed.** "I could not determine X" is a legitimate and
   valuable output. A confident wrong answer costs more than an admitted gap, and it costs it
   later, when it is more expensive.

## On the last one

This is the rule that most affects output quality, and the one most often violated silently.
A model asked to produce a complete document will produce a complete-looking document, filling
gaps with plausible material. The gaps are exactly where the reader most needs the truth.

When something cannot be determined, say so in the artifact, in the place where the answer
would have gone.
